What sin θ means here: split b into a part along a and a part sideways from a. The sideways part is the green height h = |b|·sin θ. So sin θ is the fraction of b that sticks out from a. The parallelogram is base × height = |a| · |b| · sin θ, and that area is the length of a × b. Parallel vectors (θ = 0° or 180°) have no sideways part, so the area and the cross product are 0. At 90° the whole of b is sideways, sin θ = 1, and the cross product is as long as it can get.

Where you'll meet it in the NV paper

Spin precession. A magnetic moment in a field B feels a torque μ × B. That torque is perpendicular to both the moment and the field, so it doesn't tip the spin toward B. It pushes the spin sideways, and the spin circles around B at the Larmor frequency. That circling is the precession behind Ramsey fringes and the ¹³C echo revivals in §III C. Set a = B along z and b = a tilted spin, then watch a × b: it always points around the z axis, never toward it.
